## How to calculate your cost per guest

Catering is quoted per guest, but cost is calculated per dish. Break it into four steps:

1. **Cost per serving of each dish.** Multiply each ingredient's amount per serving by the price on your latest invoice, and add them up.
2. **Food cost per guest.** Add the servings of everything on the menu: entrée, side, bread, dessert and drink.
3. **Extra food.** You always make a little more. If you prepare 10% extra, multiply the cost per guest by 1.10.
4. **Price.** Divide the food cost per guest by your target percentage (for example, 0.30), then add staff, disposables, rentals and delivery.

The full worked example, with numbers, is in our guide on [how to price catering](/en/blog/how-to-price-catering).

## Why last month's quote no longer works

The per-guest price you worked out for your last event was based on that week's prices. If chicken, cream or oil went up, that number is no longer your cost. Recosting the whole menu by hand before every quote is the step almost nobody has time for.

Teremu ties that work to something you already do: receiving invoices. Every time you scan one, the new price flows into every dish that uses that ingredient, so when you put a quote together you already have each dish's current cost.

## What to watch in catering

- **Proteins**: usually the most expensive part of the plate and the price that moves most.
- **Time between quote and purchase**: put an expiration date on your quotes and check your costs before you confirm the booking.
- **Extra food**: overproducing is normal, but count it in your cost per guest.
- **Disposables and rentals**: plates, cutlery, napkins, linens and chafing dishes are part of what each guest costs.
- **Invoice errors**: on big orders, an overcharge hurts. Teremu matches the delivery note against the invoice to catch it.
