## How to calculate the cost of a drink

In a coffee shop, every drink is a small recipe. The math takes three steps:

1. **Price per gram or ounce.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 5 lb bag of espresso beans (80 oz) for $60 is $0.75 per ounce; a gallon of whole milk (128 fl oz) for $4.48 is $0.035 per fluid ounce.
2. **Cost of the drink.** Multiply the coffee and milk in each drink by their unit price, then add the cup, lid, sleeve and anything else that goes out with it.
3. **Cost percentage.** Divide the cost by the menu price. If you want cost to be 25% of the price, divide the cost by 0.25.

Use the dose your baristas actually pull, not the one on the recipe card. Two extra grams per shot, across hundreds of drinks a day, show up at the end of the month.

## Why prices go stale

Costing a latte takes ten minutes. The hard part is redoing it when milk goes up, you change roasters or the cup supplier updates its price list. Most new coffee shops do the math once and never revisit it.

Teremu moves that work into something you already do: receiving invoices. Each time you scan one, the new price flows into every drink that uses that ingredient, and you see which drinks fell below your margin, sorted worst first.

## What to watch in a coffee shop

- **Dairy and plant-based milks**: in a milk drink, the milk can cost as much as the coffee or more.
- **Add-ons**: extra shots, syrups and whipped cream. The upcharge should cover what they cost.
- **Paper goods**: cup, lid and sleeve can add up to more than 15 cents per to-go drink.
- **Resale items**: pastries, cookies and bottled drinks carry their own margin; check it whenever your purchase price changes.
