## How to calculate the cost of a dish

A dish's cost is the sum of what each ingredient costs in the amount you serve. It takes three steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 40 lb case of chicken breast at $160 costs $4.00 per pound.
2. **Cost per plate.** Multiply the amount on the plate by that price: 8 oz of chicken is $2.00. Add every ingredient, including oil, sides and sauce.
3. **Cost percentage.** Divide the plate cost by the menu price. A dish that costs $2.86 and sells for $10 runs a 28.6% cost.

Start with your best sellers. They carry the most weight in your results.

## The number to check every week

Plate cost tells you whether the menu is designed well. **Weekly food cost** tells you whether the operation is living up to it. Calculate it as beginning inventory plus purchases minus ending inventory, divided by the week's sales. If it climbs without any menu change, something is going on: a supplier raised prices, there is waste or portions are creeping up.

## What to watch in a new restaurant

- **Proteins**: beef, chicken and seafood are usually the most expensive part of the plate and the most volatile in price.
- **Best sellers with thin margins**: one dish that sells a lot and earns little can matter more than ten that hardly anyone orders.
- **Invoice errors**: quantities that never arrived or prices that differ from what you agreed. Check each invoice against what you received.
- **Menu prices**: if you do not review them every few months, each supplier increase comes straight out of your profit.
