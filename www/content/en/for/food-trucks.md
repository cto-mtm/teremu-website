## How to calculate the cost of a food truck menu item

A food truck menu is short and the volume is high, so every cent per plate adds up. The math takes three steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: 20 lb of ground beef for $90 is $4.50 per pound.
2. **Cost of the item.** Multiply the amount each item uses by that unit price and add everything up. Include the clamshell, the bag and the sauce cups: on a truck almost everything goes out in a to-go container.
3. **Food cost percentage.** Divide the item cost by its menu price. If you want ingredients to be 30% of the price, divide the cost by 0.30.

With a menu of five or six items, you can do this in an afternoon. The hard part is redoing it every time a price changes.

## Why a notebook stops working

A food truck buys from a lot of places: the meat supplier, the warehouse club, the produce market, the restaurant supply store. Each one has its own receipt, sometimes handwritten. If you don't log those prices, your costs stay frozen on opening day.

Teremu moves that work into something you already do: keeping the receipt. Scan it with your phone, check what the AI read, and the new price flows into every menu item that uses that ingredient. If one drops below your margin, you get an alert.

## What to watch on a food truck

- **Protein and fry oil**: usually the most expensive inputs and the ones whose prices move the most.
- **To-go supplies**: clamshells, cups, bags and napkins are part of the cost of every plate you hand out.
- **What you over-prep**: food you prepped for an event and didn't sell was still paid for. Compare what you bought with what you sold.
- **Event fees and card fees**: they aren't food cost, but they come out of the same margin. An item with a tight food cost may not survive a high event fee.
