## How to calculate the cost of what you bake

In a bakery, cost is decided in ounces. A sweet dough can have ten ingredients, and each one changes price on its own schedule. That is why it helps to split the math into three steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 50 lb bag of flour at $20 costs $0.40 per pound.
2. **Cost of the full recipe.** Multiply the amount the recipe uses by that unit price, then add up every ingredient.
3. **Cost per piece.** Divide the recipe cost by the pieces that actually come out of the oven and sell, not the ones you expected. Pieces that burn, come out misshapen or go unsold still cost money.

With that cost per piece you can set the price: if you want ingredients to be 30% of the price, divide the cost by 0.30.

## Why the spreadsheet stops working

The math is not hard. The hard part is **keeping it current**. When butter goes up 10%, you have to update the price in the sheet, check every recipe that uses it and decide which prices to change. Most new bakeries do it once before opening and never touch it again.

Teremu moves that work into something you already do: receiving invoices. Every time you scan one, the new price flows into every recipe that uses that ingredient, and you see right away which products fell below your target margin.

## What to watch in a bakery

- **Butter, eggs and chocolate**: usually the most expensive ingredients and the ones whose prices move the most.
- **High-volume, low-price items**: a one-cent difference on a dinner roll or a cookie, multiplied by hundreds of pieces a day, adds up to real money every month.
- **Custom orders**: celebration cakes deserve their own costing, with decoration and packaging included.
- **Packaging**: boxes, bags and liners are part of the cost of every piece that goes out the door.
