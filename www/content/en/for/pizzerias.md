## How to calculate the cost of a pizza

A pizza looks simple: dough, sauce, cheese and a topping or two. But each one has its own price, and the cost changes with the size. It helps to split the math into three steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 20 lb case of mozzarella at $70 costs $3.50 per pound.
2. **Cost of each pizza in each size.** Multiply the ounces of each ingredient by its unit price, add everything up and add the box. A 14-inch pepperoni can cost around $4, and more than half of that is cheese.
3. **Selling price.** If you want ingredients to be 30% of the price, divide the cost by 0.30.

The guide [how much does a pizza cost to make](/en/blog/how-much-does-a-pizza-cost-to-make) walks through the full example in three sizes, and the [free tools](/en/tools) page has calculators to run the math with your own numbers.

## Why the spreadsheet stops working

The math is not hard. The hard part is **keeping it current**. When cheese goes up, the cost of almost your whole menu changes at once, in every size. Updating that by hand every week does not happen in a pizzeria with the oven running.

Teremu moves that work into something you already do: receiving invoices. Every time you scan one, the new price flows into every pizza that uses that ingredient, and you see right away which ones fell below your target margin.

## What to watch in a pizzeria

- **Cheese**: the most expensive ingredient and the one that moves the most. Weigh portions; an extra half ounce on every pie shows up at the end of the month.
- **Sizes**: a 14-inch pizza has almost twice the surface of a 10-inch. Check that the price gap follows the cost gap.
- **Premium toppings**: pepperoni, sausage, prosciutto or shrimp change the cost of a specialty pie a lot.
- **Delivery packaging**: box, bag, dipping cups and napkins are part of the cost of every order. If you sell through delivery apps, also read [what the app fees really take](/en/blog/ghost-kitchen-delivery-app-fees).
