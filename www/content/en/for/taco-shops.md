## How to calculate the cost of a taco

A taco has few ingredients, but one of them changes weight between the moment you buy it and the moment you serve it: the meat. That is why it helps to do the math in four steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 20 lb case of beef at $110 is $5.50 per pound raw.
2. **Price per pound of cooked meat.** Weigh the meat before and after cooking. If 20 lb raw gives you 13 lb cooked, your yield is 65%, and a pound of cooked meat costs $110 ÷ 13 = $8.46.
3. **Cost per taco.** Multiply the ounces of cooked meat by that price and add tortillas, salsa, onion, cilantro and lime. A taco with 2 oz of meat can cost around $1.28.
4. **Cost per plate.** Multiply by the tacos on the plate and add the sides, including the to-go container.

The guide [how to price tacos](/en/blog/how-to-price-tacos) walks through the full example, and the [free tools](/en/tools) page has calculators to run the math with your own numbers.

## How to set it up in Teremu

Teremu does not measure cooking loss; you get that number with a scale. What it does is keep prices current. If your taco has 2 oz of cooked meat and your meat yields 65%, enter about 3.1 oz of raw meat on that taco (2 ÷ 0.65). Every time you scan your meat supplier's invoice, the cost of every taco and plate that uses that meat updates on its own.

## What to watch in a taco shop

- **Meat**: most of the cost. Re-check your yield now and then, especially when you change cuts or suppliers.
- **Tortillas**: if you serve them doubled, count them twice.
- **Salsas and garnish**: onion, cilantro, limes and chiles swing with the season. They are cents per taco, but they are cents on every taco.
- **Plates and combos**: cost the whole plate and compare it with its price, not just the single taco.
- **To-go**: trays, foil, bags and salsa cups are part of the cost too.
