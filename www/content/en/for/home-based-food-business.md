## How to calculate the cost of what you sell from home

When you sell from your own kitchen, there is rarely a system: there is a note on your phone, a group chat and a rough idea of what you spent. The right calculation fits in four steps:

1. **Unit price of each ingredient.** Divide what you paid by what you bought: a 2 lb bag of chocolate chips at $14.00 costs $7.00 per pound, or about $0.44 per ounce.
2. **Cost of the full recipe.** Multiply the amount the recipe uses by that unit price and add everything up, including what was already in your pantry.
3. **Cost per portion.** Divide by the portions you actually sell, not the ones you expected, and add the packaging for each one.
4. **Price.** If you want cost to be 30% of the price, divide the cost per portion by 0.30.

The full worked example is in the guide [how to price homemade food](/en/blog/how-to-price-homemade-food). If you just want to run the numbers quickly, use the [free calculators](/en/tools).

## What gets forgotten in a home-based business

- **Packaging and labels.** Boxes, containers, bags, utensils and stickers can cost as much as an ingredient.
- **Your time.** The share of the price that is not ingredients has to pay for your hours, gas or electricity, delivery and payment app fees.
- **Portions you give away or break.** Tastings, canceled orders and pieces that do not come out right still cost money.
- **Expensive ingredients that go up.** Chocolate, butter, cheese and meat are the ones that move your cost the most.
