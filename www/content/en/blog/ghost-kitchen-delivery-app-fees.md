# How to price ghost kitchen menu items with delivery app fees

A ghost kitchen's menu prices have to cover something a dine-in restaurant doesn't pay on every sale: the delivery app's commission. If you calculate food cost against the menu price, you'll think you're making more than you are. This guide shows you how to calculate it against what you actually receive, and how to keep it current.

## Step 1: cost the whole dish, packaging included

Add up each ingredient (amount used × the unit price on your latest invoice), then add everything that goes in the bag. Here's a burger combo:

| Item | Amount | Unit price | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef | 6 oz | $4.80 / lb | $1.80 |
| Brioche bun | 1 | $0.45 each | $0.45 |
| Cheddar | 2 slices | $0.15 / slice | $0.30 |
| Fries | 8 oz | $1.00 / lb | $0.50 |
| Lettuce, tomato, onion and sauce | | | $0.85 |
| **Ingredient subtotal** | | | **$3.90** |
| Clamshell, bag, sauce cup and utensils | | | $0.60 |
| **Total dish cost** | | | **$4.50** |

Packaging looks small, but here it's 13% of the dish's cost ($0.60 ÷ $4.50).

## Step 2: work out what you actually receive

Subtract the app's commission from the menu price. If the combo sells for $14.00 and the commission is 25%:

**$14.00 × 0.25 = $3.50 commission → $14.00 − $3.50 = $10.50 net**

Commissions are often between 15% and 30% depending on the plan. Here's the same dish at each level:

| Commission | Commission in $ | Net revenue | Left after cost | Cost as % of net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15% | $2.10 | $11.90 | $7.40 | 37.8% |
| 20% | $2.80 | $11.20 | $6.70 | 40.2% |
| 25% | $3.50 | $10.50 | $6.00 | 42.9% |
| 30% | $4.20 | $9.80 | $5.30 | 45.9% |

What's left still has to cover kitchen rent, payroll, utilities and your profit.

## Step 3: compare your real percentage with your target

Against the menu price, the combo looks healthy: $4.50 ÷ $14.00 = 32.1%. Against what you receive at a 25% commission, it's 42.9%. That's your real number.

This is the most common mistake new ghost kitchens make: pricing by dine-in rules and forgetting that a quarter of every sale never arrives.

## Step 4: price from your net revenue

Decide what share of what you receive should go to ingredients and packaging. If you pick 40% and the commission is 25%:

1. **Net revenue needed** = $4.50 ÷ 0.40 = $11.25
2. **App price** = $11.25 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = $11.25 ÷ 0.75 = $15.00

Notice that 40% of what you receive equals 30% of the menu price ($4.50 ÷ $15.00), because the app keeps a quarter. If $15.00 is well above what people in your area pay for a combo like this, cutting the price isn't the only answer. You can adjust the portion, swap the side, negotiate with your supplier, or push the dishes that do leave a margin.

## Step 5: watch out for promotions

A discount you fund lowers your net revenue and raises your cost percentage, even though nothing changed in the kitchen. If you offer 20% off the $14.00 combo, the customer pays $11.20. Depending on your contract, the commission may be calculated before or after the discount. If it's calculated on the discounted price:

| | No promotion | 20% off |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pays | $14.00 | $11.20 |
| Commission (25%) | $3.50 | $2.80 |
| Net revenue | $10.50 | $8.40 |
| Dish cost | $4.50 | $4.50 |
| **You keep** | **$6.00** | **$3.90** |

A promotion can make sense if it brings orders you wouldn't otherwise get, but run the numbers before you switch it on.

## Step 6: keep it current

Supplier prices move, and in a kitchen with several brands one increase touches many dishes. If ground beef goes from $4.80 to $5.60 a pound, the 6 oz patty goes from $1.80 to $2.10. The combo goes from $4.50 to $4.80, and at a 25% commission what you keep drops from $6.00 to $5.70 per order. At 40 combos a day, that's $12 a day, about $360 a month, on one dish.

To stay on top of it:

- **Check every invoice when it arrives** and compare prices with the previous order.
- **Find which dishes use the ingredient that went up**, across all your brands.
- **Act quickly**: raise the app price, adjust the recipe, or switch suppliers.
