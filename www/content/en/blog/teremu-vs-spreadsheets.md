# Spreadsheet or app: what should you use to track food costs?

**Short answer:** if you have a short menu, a couple of vendors and prices that barely change, Excel (or any spreadsheet) is enough. If you buy several times a week, prices move, and you don't have time to type in every invoice, an app like Teremu takes that work off you and keeps your costs current on its own. Here's the full comparison, without overselling either side.

## When a spreadsheet is enough

A spreadsheet is a great tool, and plenty of people have used one well for years. It's enough when:

- **You sell few items.** A short menu of ten or fifteen dishes can be costed in an afternoon.
- **Your prices rarely change.** If you buy from one or two vendors with stable prices, the sheet ages slowly.
- **You enjoy it.** If you like building formulas and checking numbers, a sheet gives you full control and costs next to nothing.
- **You work alone, on a computer.** Nobody else needs to see or edit your costs.

If that's you, you don't need an app. A good recipe costing template and the habit of updating it will do.

## Where the spreadsheet breaks

The problem isn't the math. Formulas are easy. The problem is **keeping the data current**.

- **Prices go stale.** A recipe cost is only as good as the last price you typed. If oil went up three weeks ago and you didn't update it, every margin is off.
- **Every invoice line has to be typed.** Ten invoices of fifteen lines a week is 150 lines someone has to copy by hand. In practice, it stops happening.
- **One ingredient lives in many recipes.** Onions, cream or cheese show up in dozens of dishes. If each recipe holds its own copy of the price, updating one doesn't update the rest.
- **Errors don't announce themselves.** A broken cell reference, a deleted row or a kilo entered as a gram produce a number that looks normal and is wrong.
- **Your team can't use it well.** Sharing the file with your chef or buyer without them seeing what they shouldn't, or breaking a formula, is hard.
- **It's awkward on a phone.** Invoices arrive in the kitchen, not the office. Editing a big sheet on a phone with your hands full is painful.

## Which should you pick?

**Stick with the spreadsheet if:**

- Your menu is short and your prices barely change.
- You have the time (and the will) to enter every invoice.
- You work alone and don't need alerts.

**Pick Teremu if:**

- You buy several times a week and prices move.
- You've stopped updating your sheet because there's no time.
- The same ingredient shows up in many recipes.
- You want your team to scan invoices from their phones without touching your numbers.
- You want to hear about a price increase when it happens, not at month end.

There's a middle path that works well: start on the Free plan, scan your invoices, and compare Teremu's costs with your sheet's for a month. Then decide with your own numbers.

## How to move from a spreadsheet to Teremu without losing anything

1. **Scan last month's invoices.** Photograph every vendor invoice. As you review them in Triage, Teremu builds your ingredient list with current prices and your vendor directory. You don't need to copy prices from your sheet.
2. **Re-enter your recipes as ingredients and quantities.** Open your sheet and, for each dish, enter the ingredients with the quantity each one uses. Teremu already has the prices.
3. **Set your menu price and target margin.** That shows you the actual margin of each dish against the one you want.
4. **Compare.** Keep your sheet as a backup for a few weeks. When a number doesn't match, it's usually a stale price in the sheet.
5. **Keep scanning.** From then on, every new invoice updates your costs on its own.
