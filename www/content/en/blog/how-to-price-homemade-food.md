# How to price homemade food to sell

If you sell desserts, meal prep or made-to-order dishes from your own kitchen, your price most likely came from what others charge online or a quick sum on your phone. The problem is that sum almost never includes everything: the ingredients you already had, packaging, the pieces you give away and your time. This guide shows you how to calculate the full cost, using a pan of brownies as the example.

## Step 1: get the unit price of each ingredient

Take the grocery receipt or the supplier invoice and divide what you paid by the amount you bought. Work each ingredient in one unit (pound, ounce or each).

| Ingredient | Purchase | You paid | Unit price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semisweet chocolate | 2 lb bag | $14.00 | $7.00 / lb |
| Butter | 1 lb | $5.00 | $5.00 / lb |
| Sugar | 4 lb bag | $3.60 | $0.90 / lb |
| Eggs | 18 count | $4.50 | $0.25 / egg |
| Flour | 5 lb bag | $4.00 | $0.80 / lb |
| Walnuts | 1 lb | $9.00 | $9.00 / lb |

If something was already in your pantry, use the price from the last time you bought it. It is not free: you will have to buy it again.

## Step 2: cost the full recipe

Multiply the amount of each ingredient by its unit price and add them up. This recipe makes a pan of 16 brownies:

| Ingredient | Amount | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Semisweet chocolate | 12 oz (0.75 lb) | $5.25 |
| Butter | 8 oz (0.5 lb) | $2.50 |
| Sugar | 8 oz (0.5 lb) | $0.45 |
| Eggs | 4 | $1.00 |
| Flour | 6 oz (0.375 lb) | $0.30 |
| Walnuts | 4 oz (0.25 lb) | $2.25 |
| **Recipe total** | | **$11.75** |

Then add what usually gets forgotten: salt, vanilla, cocoa for dusting and the parchment or spray for the pan.

## Step 3: divide by the portions you actually sell and add packaging

The pan makes 16 pieces, but one goes to tasting or comes out broken from the edge. Divide by 15:

**$11.75 ÷ 15 = $0.78 per brownie**

Now add the packaging for each piece. If the individual box and label cost $0.30:

**$0.78 + $0.30 = $1.08 total cost per brownie**

## Step 4: set the price with your target percentage

Decide what share of the price you want cost to be. Many food businesses work with an ingredient cost of roughly 25% to 35% of the price. If you choose 30%:

**Price = $1.08 ÷ 0.30 = $3.60**

## Step 5: check that the price pays for your time

In a home-based business, the 70% that is not cost has to pay for your hours, the gas or electricity for the oven, delivery and payment app fees. Check whether it is enough. If baking, cutting and packing the pan takes you 2 hours:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sales from the pan (15 × $3.60) | $54.00 |
| Total cost (15 × $1.08) | $16.20 |
| **What is left** | **$37.80** |
| **Per hour (÷ 2 hours)** | **$18.90** |

That $18.90 an hour still has to cover gas, delivery and fees. If the number does not work for you, you have three levers: raise the price, make bigger batches in the same time, or find a better price on the expensive ingredients.

## The most common mistake: counting only what you bought for that order

Most people who sell from home calculate with that day's receipt: in this case, the chocolate and walnuts, which add up to $7.50 in the recipe. But the butter, sugar, flour and eggs you already had also went into it. In this example, counting only those two items leaves out $4.25 of the $11.75, more than a third of the real cost. Add forgotten packaging and pieces that do not sell, and a price that looked good may be leaving very little.

## Keep it current

Grocery prices change often. If chocolate goes from $7.00 to $9.00 a pound, the recipe goes from $11.75 to $13.25, and the total cost per brownie from $1.08 to $1.18. At the same $3.60 price, your cost goes from 30.0% to 32.8% of the price. It does not look like much on one piece, but it repeats on every pan, and it is rarely just one ingredient going up.

To stay on top of it:

- **Keep every receipt** and compare prices with your previous purchase.
- **Find which products use the ingredient that went up.** Butter and eggs are in almost everything.
- **Decide quickly.** Adjust the price, the size or the recipe, but do not let it ride for months.
