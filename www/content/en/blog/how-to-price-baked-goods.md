# How to price baked goods

When you open a bakery, the price of each item usually comes from two places: what the competition charges and a quick calculation you did the first month. Both fail for the same reason: they do not follow the real price of your ingredients. This guide shows you how to calculate it properly and keep it current.

## Step 1: get the unit price of each ingredient

Take the latest invoice from each supplier and divide what you paid by the amount you bought. Work in one unit per ingredient (pound, ounce or each).

| Ingredient | Purchase | You paid | Unit price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 50 lb bag | $20.00 | $0.40 / lb |
| Butter | 36 lb case | $108.00 | $3.00 / lb |
| Sugar | 50 lb bag | $30.00 | $0.60 / lb |
| Eggs | 15 dozen (180) | $36.00 | $0.20 / egg |
| Yeast | 2 lb | $8.00 | $0.25 / oz |

## Step 2: cost the full recipe

Multiply the amount of each ingredient in the recipe by its unit price and add them up. Here is a batch of 12 large sweet rolls:

| Ingredient | Amount | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | 2 lb | $0.80 |
| Butter | 1 lb | $3.00 |
| Sugar | 1 lb | $0.60 |
| Eggs | 3 | $0.60 |
| Yeast | 1 oz | $0.25 |
| **Recipe total** | | **$5.25** |

Then add what usually gets forgotten: salt, milk, vanilla, cinnamon, the glaze or frosting, and the packaging for each piece.

## Step 3: divide by the pieces you actually sell

The batch makes 12 rolls, but on average you sell 11 (one comes out misshapen or is left over). Divide by 11:

**$5.25 ÷ 11 = $0.48 per roll**

This is the most common mistake: dividing by the ideal yield makes every piece look cheaper than it really is. Dividing by 12 would give you $0.44, and you would be pricing off a number that never happens.

## Step 4: set the price with your target percentage

Decide what share of the price you want ingredients to be. If you choose 30%:

**Price = $0.48 ÷ 0.30 = $1.60**

Then compare it with your market. If your calculated price lands well above what people pay nearby, lowering the price is not always the answer: you might change the size, the recipe or the supplier instead.

## Step 5: keep it current

This is where almost every bakery slips. If butter goes from $3.00 to $3.50 a pound, that batch goes from $5.25 to $5.75, and each roll from $0.48 to $0.52. It looks small, but on a product you sell 200 times a day that is about $9 a day, more than $270 a month, from a single recipe.

To stay on top of it:

- **Check invoices when they arrive.** Compare each price with the previous order.
- **Find which products use the ingredient that went up.** Butter and eggs are in almost everything.
- **Decide quickly.** Raise the price, adjust the recipe or switch suppliers, but do not let it ride for months.
