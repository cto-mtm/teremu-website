# How much does a pizza cost to make?

A 14-inch pepperoni pizza can cost around $4 in ingredients and box, and more than half of that is usually cheese. But that number changes with your suppliers, your portions and the size. This guide shows you how to calculate the real cost of each pizza, size by size, and keep it current when cheese goes up.

## Step 1: get the unit price of each ingredient

Take the latest invoice from each supplier and divide what you paid by the amount you bought. Work in pounds, ounces, fluid ounces or each.

| Ingredient | Purchase | You paid | Unit price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 50 lb bag | $25.00 | $0.50 / lb |
| Mozzarella | 20 lb case | $70.00 | $3.50 / lb |
| Pizza sauce | 6 lb can | $4.80 | $0.80 / lb |
| Pepperoni | 12.5 lb case | $50.00 | $4.00 / lb |
| Olive oil | 1 gal (128 fl oz) | $25.60 | $0.20 / fl oz |
| 14-inch boxes | 50 | $22.50 | $0.45 each |

Do the same with the boxes for each size. In this example, a 10-inch box costs $0.35 and a 12-inch box $0.40.

## Step 2: cost each size separately

Weigh what actually goes on each pizza: the flour in the dough ball, the sauce, the cheese and each topping. Then multiply by the unit price. A pepperoni pizza in three sizes:

| Ingredient | Small (10") | Medium (12") | Large (14") |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 4 oz · $0.13 | 6 oz · $0.19 | 8 oz · $0.25 |
| Olive oil | 0.25 fl oz · $0.05 | 0.4 fl oz · $0.08 | 0.5 fl oz · $0.10 |
| Sauce | 3 oz · $0.15 | 4.5 oz · $0.23 | 6 oz · $0.30 |
| Mozzarella | 5 oz · $1.09 | 7 oz · $1.53 | 10 oz · $2.19 |
| Pepperoni | 1.5 oz · $0.38 | 2 oz · $0.50 | 3 oz · $0.75 |
| Box | $0.35 | $0.40 | $0.45 |
| **Total cost** | **$2.15** | **$2.93** | **$4.04** |

Two things stand out. First, cheese is more than half the cost of the large ($2.19 of $4.04). Second, the box is about 16% of the cost of the small. Yeast, salt, oregano and bench flour are pennies, but add them too if you want precision.

## Step 3: set the price with your target percentage

Decide what share of the price you want ingredients to be. Many pizzerias work with roughly 25% to 35%. If you choose 30%, divide the cost by 0.30:

| Size | Cost | Price at 30% |
|---|---|---|
| Small | $2.15 | $7.17 |
| Medium | $2.93 | $9.77 |
| Large | $4.04 | $13.47 |

Then round to prices that make sense on your menu and compare them with your market. If the calculated price lands well above what people pay nearby, lowering it is not always the answer: you can adjust the portion, the recipe or the supplier.

## The most common mistake: stepping sizes up by a fixed amount

Many pizzerias price the large by adding a fixed amount to the medium. The problem is that cost does not grow with diameter, it grows with surface area. A 14-inch pizza has almost twice the surface of a 10-inch (about 154 square inches versus 79), so it takes almost twice the dough, sauce and cheese.

Here is what happens if you sell the small at $7.99, the medium at $9.99 and the large at $11.99:

| Size | Cost | Price | Cost as % of price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | $2.15 | $7.99 | 26.9% |
| Medium | $2.93 | $9.99 | 29.3% |
| Large | $4.04 | $11.99 | 33.7% |

The large, usually the one people order to share, is the one that earns the least. Giving a better deal on the big size is fine, but it should be a decision, not an accident.

The other frequent mistake is not weighing cheese. If every cook eyeballs it and goes one ounce over on each large, that is $0.22 more per pizza that was never in your math.

## Step 4: keep it current

This is where almost every pizzeria slips. Cheese changes price often, and because it is on almost everything, one increase moves your whole menu at once.

If mozzarella goes from $3.50 to $4.00 a pound:

- The large goes from $4.04 to $4.35 (+$0.31).
- The medium goes from $2.93 to $3.15 (+$0.22).
- The small goes from $2.15 to $2.31 (+$0.16).

With the large at $13.47, your cost goes from 30% to about 32%. If you sell 40 larges a day, that is $12.50 a day, about $375 a month, from the cheese on one size.

To stay on top of it:

- **Check every invoice when it arrives.** Compare the price of cheese, flour and boxes with the previous order.
- **Recalculate every size.** A cheese increase hits every pizza on the menu, not just your best seller.
- **Decide quickly.** Raise the price, adjust the portion or switch suppliers, but do not let it ride for months.
