# How to price tacos

A meat taco can cost a little over a dollar in ingredients, and most of it is meat. But a taco shop sells hundreds a day at a low price, so a mistake of a few cents per taco turns into hundreds of dollars a month. This guide shows you how to calculate the real cost of every taco and every plate, and how to keep it current when meat goes up.

## Step 1: get the unit price of each ingredient

Take the latest invoice from each supplier and divide what you paid by what you bought.

| Ingredient | Purchase | You paid | Unit price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beef flap meat | 20 lb case | $110.00 | $5.50 / lb raw |
| Corn tortillas, street size | pack of 80 | $4.00 | $0.05 each |
| Onions | 50 lb sack | $30.00 | $0.60 / lb |
| Cilantro | 60 bunches | $30.00 | $0.50 / bunch |
| Limes | 200 | $30.00 | $0.15 each |
| Salsa verde (your batch) | 1 gal (128 fl oz) | $6.40 in ingredients | $0.05 / fl oz |

For the salsa, add up what the tomatillos, chiles, onion and the rest of one batch cost you and divide by the fluid ounces it makes.

## Step 2: work out the price of cooked meat

This is the step almost everyone skips. You buy meat raw, but it goes on the taco cooked, and it loses water and fat on the grill. Weigh a full purchase before and after cooking:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Raw meat | 20 lb |
| Cooked meat | 13 lb |
| Yield | 13 ÷ 20 = 65% |
| You paid | $110.00 |
| **Cost per cooked pound** | **$110.00 ÷ 13 = $8.46 (about $0.53 / oz)** |

Yield depends on the cut, how much you trim and how you cook it. Al pastor, carnitas, barbacoa or chicken all yield differently, so measure each one separately and repeat the measurement now and then.

## Step 3: cost one taco

Now multiply each ingredient by its unit price. A carne asada taco on double tortillas:

| Ingredient | Amount | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked meat | 2 oz | $1.06 |
| Tortillas | 2 | $0.10 |
| Onion | 0.5 oz | $0.02 |
| Cilantro | 1/25 bunch | $0.02 |
| Salsa verde | 1 fl oz | $0.05 |
| Lime | 1 wedge (1/6 lime) | $0.03 |
| **Cost per taco** | | **$1.28** |

Meat is more than 80% of the cost ($1.06 of $1.28). Everything else together is 22 cents.

## Step 4: set the price by the piece and by the plate

If you want ingredients to be 30% of the price, divide the cost by 0.30: $1.28 ÷ 0.30 ≈ $4.27. Rounded to $4.25, your cost is 30.1% of the price.

Now the plate. If you sell a plate of 3 tacos for $11.00 (instead of $12.75 for 3 singles):

| Sale | Cost | Price | Cost as % of price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single taco | $1.28 | $4.25 | 30.1% |
| Plate of 3, dine-in | $3.84 | $11.00 | 34.9% |
| Plate of 3, to-go (+$0.25 container and foil) | $4.09 | $11.00 | 37.2% |

A plate discount is fine, but it has to fit inside your margin. If plates are what you sell most, that is the number that matters. And if the plate comes with rice and beans, cost those too.

## The most common mistake: costing with raw meat

If you use the raw price ($5.50 a pound), the 2 oz of meat look like $0.69 instead of $1.06, and the whole taco like $0.91 instead of $1.28. With the taco at $4.25, you would think your cost is 21.4% when it is really 30.1%. That is 37 cents per taco you never see, because the scale is not in the spreadsheet.

The second mistake is portioning meat by eye. If each taco gets 2.5 oz instead of 2 oz, that is 26 cents more per taco at $8.46 a cooked pound.

## Step 5: keep it current

Meat changes price often, and in a taco shop a small change gets multiplied by a lot of volume. If raw beef goes from $5.50 to $6.00 a pound:

- A cooked pound goes from $8.46 to $9.23.
- The taco goes from $1.28 to $1.37, almost 10 cents more.
- At $4.25, your cost goes from 30.1% to 32.2%.
- The dine-in plate of 3 goes from $3.84 to $4.11, 37.4% of its price.

If you sell 300 tacos a day, those cents add up to about $29 a day, more than $850 a month.

To stay on top of it:

- **Check every meat invoice when it arrives.** Compare the price per pound with the previous order.
- **Recalculate the taco and the plate.** The plate is usually tighter than the single taco.
- **Decide quickly.** Raise the price a few cents, adjust the portion or switch suppliers, but do not let it ride for months.
